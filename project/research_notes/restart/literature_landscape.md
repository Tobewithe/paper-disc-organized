## Current project anchor

`RESEARCH_BRIEF.md` describes a YOLO26-seg diagnosis-to-improvement study on PigLife, FaroPigSeg and BamaPig2D. Existing cache diagnostics report 7,374 GT, baseline failure rates of 8.81% (PigLife), 45.49% (FaroPigSeg), and 23.69% (BamaPig2D). In the fixed cache, Mask-NMS at 0.80 changed the error mix and converted 427/675 O-labelled GT, but residual low-overlap cases remained. A 50-case `SPLIT_TYPE` slice had median joint-IoU gain 0.084 and coverage gain 0.143 when predictions were unioned; 31/50 had complete and local candidates jointly retained. These are descriptive, cache-bound observations. They do not establish causality, modal/amodal semantics, or generalization.

## Mechanism classification and gap map

1. **Proposal/query formation and assignment.** DETR/OTA/DINO/Mask DINO establish that assignment and query initialization can be global and one-to-one. The project has not yet measured assignment loss before mask-head loss on the same forward pass. A missing full candidate, a retained local candidate, and a mask that is intrinsically incomplete must be separated with raw traces and controlled perturbations.

2. **Selection/ranking/NMS/duplicate.** SOLOv2/Matrix NMS and DETR remove or reduce duplicate handling, while the 2026 topology-aware preprint makes fixed-candidate set construction an explicit relational problem. The project’s NMS scan rules out ordinary high-overlap duplicate suppression as a sufficient explanation, but does not rule out score calibration, Top-K truncation, or structured selection. Generic relational selection is not a clean novelty claim after arXiv:2608.11607.

3. **Mask construction and instance separation.** CondInst, PointRend, layering and CClusnet-Inseg show distinct ways to represent instance masks or separate touching objects. None provides a controlled decomposition of the project’s low-overlap complete/local cases. A union oracle is an upper bound, not a deployable method and not proof that masks should be merged.

4. **Occlusion, visible/amodal and layering.** BCNet, ORM, Relook and OOSIS model occlusion relations or order. They require annotation semantics and visible boundary evidence. “Touching” in the current labels cannot be treated as “occluded,” and modal GT cannot support amodal claims without annotation verification.

5. **Dense agricultural/animal benchmarks.** Pig-specific work demonstrates that realistic occlusion and dense housing are material, and that center-based representations can be robust. Existing benchmarks differ in label schema, camera/viewpoint and metrics; cross-dataset AP alone cannot establish the same failure mechanism.

## What the literature supports, and what it does not

Supported: dense overlap makes contour/occlusion boundaries ambiguous; global assignment, explicit relations, center/layer representations and adaptive mask refinement are established mechanism families; pig settings expose occlusion and visibility conditions omitted by many generic benchmarks.

Not supported by the literature alone: that candidate ranking is the dominant cause in this project; that a relational selector will improve YOLO26; that local complementary masks should be merged; that all touching pigs are occluded; that modal annotations encode amodal extent; or that gains on COCO, KINS, cells, surgical instruments or piglet datasets transfer to PigLife/FaroPigSeg/BamaPig2D.

## Novelty and risk assessment

- **High conflict risk:** generic graph/topology-aware candidate selection (arXiv:2608.11607) and generic query recombination/alignment for overlap (arXiv:2608.29253).
- **Moderate conflict risk:** explicit bilayer/layering/occlusion-order mechanisms (BCNet, Layering, OOSIS); novelty would need to be pig-specific, diagnosis-driven, or experimentally bounded rather than architectural wording alone.
- **Lower but unresolved risk:** a causal, reproducible failure taxonomy linking raw YOLO26 candidate formation, score/rank retention, mask construction and pig-specific density strata. I found no verified paper that performs this exact cross-stage analysis on the three project datasets.
